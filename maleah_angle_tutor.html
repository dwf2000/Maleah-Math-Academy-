📚 Understanding Angle Relationships

What are Angles?

An angle is formed when two lines (called rays) meet at a point. We measure angles in degrees (°).

Imagine opening a door. When it's closed, the angle is 0°. Open it a little = small angle. Open it all the way against the wall = 180°. The amount you open the door is the angle!

🎯 The Key Concepts You Need to Know

1. Angles on a Straight Line = 180°

When angles sit on a straight line, they ALWAYS add up to exactly 180 degrees. This is called being supplementary.

Think of a straight line like a seesaw that's perfectly flat. If one side takes up some room (angle a), the other side gets whatever's left to make 180°. It's like sharing 180° of pizza - what one angle takes, the other loses!
Supplementary Angles Formula
Angle 1 + Angle 2 = 180°

If you know one angle, subtract it from 180° to find the other!

2. Angles Around a Point = 360°

When multiple angles meet at a single point, they ALWAYS add up to 360 degrees (a full circle).

Think of a full spin on a skateboard or doing a complete twirl - that's 360°! If you're standing at a point and look in all directions, you've looked through 360°. All the angles around you have to share that full circle.

3. Vertical (Opposite) Angles are Equal

When two lines cross, they create two pairs of vertical angles. Vertical angles are ALWAYS equal!

When two roads cross, the opposite corners are like twins - they're exactly the same size! If you're standing at a crossroads, looking forward and looking backward gives you the same angle.

4. Right Angles = 90°

A right angle is exactly 90 degrees. It looks like the corner of a book or a square. We mark it with a small square symbol.

5. Complementary Angles = 90°

Two angles that add up to 90° are called complementary angles.

If you have a right angle (like the corner of your desk) and you put a line through it, the two smaller angles must add up to 90°. They "complete" each other to make a right angle!

🧮 Working with Ratios

What is a Ratio?

A ratio compares how things relate to each other using numbers with colons. For example, 4:5 means "for every 4 parts of one thing, there are 5 parts of another."

Imagine you and your friend are sharing a pizza. If the ratio is 4:5, you get 4 slices for every 5 slices your friend gets. The TOTAL slices would be 4 + 5 = 9 slices. If you have 18 slices total, you'd get 8 and your friend would get 10!
Solving Ratio Problems
If ratio is 4:5, angles are 4x and 5x
4x + 5x = 180° → 9x = 180° → x = 20°
Angles: 4(20°) = 80° and 5(20°) = 100°

🔧 Tips & Tricks

Always identify what you know first! Look at the diagram carefully. What angles are given? What are you trying to find?
Look for the magic numbers: 180° (straight line) and 360° (full rotation). If angles are on a line, they add to 180°. If they're around a point, they add to 360°.
Use variables when angles aren't given. If you see "3x°" and "2x°", set up an equation. The x is like a mystery number you need to find!
Check your work! After solving, plug your answer back in. Do all the angles add up correctly?

Practice Problem 1 (Similar to #1)

Find ∠DAE. The right angle is at A (90°), and ∠EAF = 70°.

1What we know: Right angle ∠CAD = 90°, and ∠EAF = 70°
2Set up: ∠DAE + ∠EAF = 90° (both angles inside the right angle)
3Solve: ∠DAE + 70° = 90° → ∠DAE = 90° - 70° = 20°
✓ ∠DAE = 20°

Practice Problem 2 (Similar to #2)

Angles on a straight line: f°, 160°, f°. Find f.

1What we know: Three angles on a line = 180°
2Set up: f° + 160° + f° = 180°
3Combine: 2f° + 160° = 180°
4Solve: 2f° = 20° → f = 10°
✓ f = 10°

Practice Problem 3 (Similar to #3)

On a line: 15°, 2x°, 100°, 3x°, 10°. Find ∠CQD (2x°) and ∠EQF (3x°).

1What we know: All angles = 180°
2Set up: 15° + 2x° + 100° + 3x° + 10° = 180°
3Combine: 125° + 5x° = 180°
4Solve: 5x° = 55° → x = 11°
5Find angles: ∠CQD = 2(11°) = 22°, ∠EQF = 3(11°) = 33°
✓ ∠CQD = 22° and ∠EQF = 33°

Practice Problem 4 (Similar to #4)

Around a point: 3 angles of 65° and 3 angles of x°. Find x.

1What we know: Angles around a point = 360°
2Set up: 3(65°) + 3x° = 360°
3Simplify: 195° + 3x° = 360°
4Solve: 3x° = 165° → x = 55°
✓ x = 55°
