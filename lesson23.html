Lesson 23: Solving Equations Using Algebra

📚 Understanding Equations

🎭 The Balance Scale Analogy

Imagine a balance scale (like the ones Lady Justice holds). An equation is just like that scale — both sides MUST be equal, or it tips over!

When you have 2x + 5 = 15, think of it as:

  • Left side of scale: 2 mystery boxes + 5 pounds
  • Right side of scale: 15 pounds

The Golden Rule: Whatever you do to one side, you MUST do to the other side to keep it balanced!

🔑 Key Vocabulary

Variable: A letter (like x, p, or m) that represents an unknown number we're trying to find.

Equation: A math statement saying two things are equal (has an = sign).

Solve: Find the value of the variable that makes the equation true.

Inverse Operations: Operations that "undo" each other:

  • Addition ↔ Subtraction (opposites)
  • Multiplication ↔ Division (opposites)

🎯 The Strategy: Work Backwards!

🎁 The Wrapped Present Analogy

Think of the variable (x) as a present that's been wrapped up with operations. To find what's inside, you need to unwrap it in reverse order!

If someone:

  1. Put a gift in a box
  2. Wrapped it with paper
  3. Tied a ribbon on it

You'd unwrap it by:

  1. Untying the ribbon (LAST thing added = FIRST thing removed)
  2. Removing the paper
  3. Opening the box

📋 The Order of Unwrapping

1
Simplify each side (if needed) — combine like terms, distribute
2
Undo addition or subtraction FIRST — move constants away from the variable
3
Undo multiplication or division LAST — isolate the variable completely
4
Check your answer! — plug it back in to verify

📝 If-Then Statements

Your teacher wants you to JUSTIFY each step. Here's how:

If-Then Statement: Explains WHY a step is valid.

Example: "If 5 is added to a number and the result is 12, then the number is 12 - 5 = 7"

Common justifications:

  • "If [something] is added to both sides, the equation remains balanced."
  • "If both sides are divided by [number], the equation remains balanced."
  • "If [number] equals [expression], then subtracting [value] from both sides gives..."

✏️ Worked Examples

Example 1: Youth Group Trip (Problem 1a-c)

Problem: The youth group is going on a trip to an amusement park. The trip costs each group member $150, which includes $85 for the hotel and TWO one-day combination entrance and meal plan passes.

Let P = the cost of one park pass

Part a: Write the Equation

What do we know?

  • Total cost: $150
  • Hotel: $85
  • Park passes: 2 of them, each costs P

The parts must equal the total:

85 + 2P = 150

Or equivalently:

85 + P + P = 150

Part b: Solve with If-Then Statements

1

Start: 85 + 2P = 150

If 85 is added to 2P and the result is 150, then 2P alone equals 150 - 85.

Subtract 85 from both sides: 2P = 150 - 85

Simplify: 2P = 65

2

Now: 2P = 65

If 2 times P equals 65, then P equals 65 ÷ 2.

Divide both sides by 2: P = 65 ÷ 2

Simplify: P = 32.50

✓

Answer: Each park pass costs $32.50

Check: $85 + $32.50 + $32.50 = $85 + $65 = $150 ✓

Example 2: Ice Cream & Water Bottles (Problem 1d-f)

Problem: You buy an ice cream bar ($2.89) and 3 bottles of water at the amusement park. You pay with a $10 bill and receive NO change.

Let W = the cost of one water bottle

Part d: Write the Equation

What do we know?

  • Total spent: $10 (no change back)
  • Ice cream: $2.89
  • Water bottles: 3 of them, each costs W
2.89 + 3W = 10

Part e: Solve with If-Then Statements

1

Start: 2.89 + 3W = 10

If $2.89 is added to 3W and the total is $10, then 3W equals $10 - $2.89.

Subtract 2.89 from both sides: 3W = 10 - 2.89

Simplify: 3W = 7.11

2

Now: 3W = 7.11

If 3 times W equals $7.11, then W equals $7.11 ÷ 3.

Divide both sides by 3: W = 7.11 ÷ 3

Simplify: W = 2.37

✓

Answer: Each water bottle costs $2.37

Check: $2.89 + (3 × $2.37) = $2.89 + $7.11 = $10.00 ✓

Example 3: Solving (2/3)x - 4 = 20

1

Start: (2/3)x - 4 = 20

If 4 is subtracted from (2/3)x and the result is 20, then (2/3)x equals 20 + 4.

Add 4 to both sides: (2/3)x = 24

2

Now: (2/3)x = 24

If (2/3) of x equals 24, then x equals 24 × (3/2). (Multiply by the reciprocal!)

Multiply both sides by 3/2: x = 24 × (3/2)

Simplify: x = 72/2 = 36

✓

Answer: x = 36

Check: (2/3)(36) - 4 = 24 - 4 = 20 ✓

💡 Pro Tip: When you have a fraction times x (like 2/3 × x), multiply by the RECIPROCAL (flip the fraction: 3/2) to cancel it out!

Example 4: Solving 12(x + 9) = -108

1

Start: 12(x + 9) = -108

If 12 times (x + 9) equals -108, then (x + 9) equals -108 ÷ 12.

Divide both sides by 12: x + 9 = -108 ÷ 12

Simplify: x + 9 = -9

2

Now: x + 9 = -9

If 9 is added to x and the result is -9, then x equals -9 - 9.

Subtract 9 from both sides: x = -9 - 9

Simplify: x = -18

✓

Answer: x = -18

Check: 12(-18 + 9) = 12(-9) = -108 ✓

Problem 5: The Pipe Problem

A plumber has a pipe cut into two sections. One section is 12 ft shorter than the other. If 3/4 of the length of the shorter pipe is 120 ft, how long is the longer piece of pipe?


Solution:

1

Let S = shorter pipe length

Given: (3/4)S = 120

Multiply by 4/3: S = 120 × (4/3) = 160 ft

2

Longer pipe = Shorter pipe + 12

L = 160 + 12 = 172 ft

Answer: The longer pipe is 172 ft

Problem 6: Bob's Phone Bill

Bob's monthly phone bill is made up of a $10 fee plus $0.05 per minute. Bob's phone bill for July was $22. How many minutes did Bob use?


Solution:

1

Let m = number of minutes

Equation: 10 + 0.05m = 22

2

Subtract 10: 0.05m = 12

3

Divide by 0.05: m = 12 ÷ 0.05 = 240

Answer: Bob used 240 minutes

Problem 7: Kym's Phone Plan

Kym switched cell phone plans. Her new plan saves her $3.50 per month compared to her old plan. The new plan costs $294 for the entire year. How much did Kym pay per month under her OLD plan?


Solution:

1

New plan yearly cost: $294

New plan monthly cost: $294 ÷ 12 = $24.50

2

New plan saves $3.50/month compared to old plan

So old plan was MORE expensive by $3.50

Old = New + $3.50 = $24.50 + $3.50 = $28.00

Answer: Kym paid $28.00 per month on her old plan
